The artwork carries the experience. The interface has a coherent visual language, but its hierarchy, selection states, and floating components are still below the requested standard. Light mode is consistently stronger. Dark mode loses too much definition between surfaces and controls.

1. **Dark comments — 6.5/10.** The selected conversation appears twice: in the sidebar and in a large floating thread. That duplicates attention and text. The long connector cuts across the roof, while the blue pin plus wireframe cube confuses annotation focus with voxel selection. Timestamps, coordinates, “Agent details,” and Resolve share similarly weak emphasis. The black composer looks punched into the charcoal card; its tiny Reply button feels disconnected from the input.

2. **Dark history — 6.8/10.** The preview banner establishes the mode clearly. The slider and separate marker row create two representations of position that users must visually reconcile. “All edits” shows only a slice of the sequence without a strong scroll-container boundary; “Scroll to explore” compensates with instructional copy. Generic Human/Agent attribution discards collaborator identity. Place still appears available during historical preview, and “Save current” does not clarify whether it saves the previewed state or the latest document.

3. **Dark menu — 7.0/10.** Menu grouping is sensible, but the Export parent loses its active treatment while its submenu is open, weakening the navigation path. The bright outline around PNG reads more like keyboard focus than a coherent menu highlight. Shortcut glyphs are substantially quieter than item labels. The floating charcoal menus, blue-tinted sidebar, and black form surfaces do not yet feel like one material system.

4. **Dark palettes — 6.7/10.** The immutable-preset policy is repeated through the subtitle, disabled controls, explanatory sentence, and duplication action. That is excessive explanation for a simple state. The double selection outline dominates the swatch grid. An enabled-looking overflow beside an otherwise locked color editor creates uncertainty about available actions. The large header and scattered footer content stretch a sparse task across an oversized dialog.

5. **Dark share — 6.6/10.** Public-link access, link capabilities, individual permissions, and presence are packed into one similarly weighted panel. “Can remix” does not explain its relationship to commenting, especially when a named collaborator separately has “Can comment.” Those are permission questions the visual hierarchy should resolve. Selected segments and inputs become heavy black cavities. “Copied” and “Link copied to clipboard” repeat the same feedback; the latter also extends an already tall popover.

6. **Dark tools — 6.4/10.** The picker combines tool navigation, extension attribution, editable settings, and scope information in one long popup. Selecting a tool and configuring a brush need clearer separation. Six conspicuously empty leading-icon slots create a broad gutter used only by the custom tool’s checkmark. The density slider is short relative to the popup width, while the seed input receives disproportionate visual weight. “Custom,” “Agent,” and “Agent tool” over-label one extension.

7. **Dark main — 7.0/10.** The session panel has too many competing status levels: Agent session, Live, task title, agent name, follow state, and current operation. The current operation should be easier to find immediately. The selected Canopy layer is barely distinguishable, while the selected swatch has a much stronger outline. Captured views are too small to inspect meaningful voxel changes. The sidebar reads as a painted gradient rather than convincing translucent material; the dark grid nearly disappears.

8. **Light comments — 6.8/10.** The card separates cleanly from the canvas, but the duplicated sidebar/thread content remains the dominant problem. The long blue connector competes with the artwork rather than quietly establishing an anchor. Author identity and message boundaries need stronger rhythm; the thread reads as a continuous stack of similarly styled text. The coordinate/Resolve row is visually detached from both the discussion and composer. Canopy’s layer selection is effectively invisible in this state.

9. **Light history — 7.1/10.** The preview boundary and banner are more legible than in dark mode. The selected history item nevertheless relies on a faint fill and short blue rule; the current step deserves a clearer relationship to the timeline. Previous/Next controls are visually weaker than surrounding metadata. Human/Agent categories identify actor type without identifying the actor. The broad empty lower panel makes the cramped, partially visible edit sequence above feel poorly allocated.

10. **Light menu — 7.4/10.** This is the strongest floating-component treatment: readable surfaces, restrained shadow, and clear separators. The missing active treatment on Export still breaks parent-to-submenu continuity. The submenu’s blue perimeter treatment feels inconsistent with the filled File trigger. Shortcut glyphs and menu labels have noticeably different visual density. The generous submenu width leaves substantial empty space after short labels, making the menu system feel less precisely fitted than the reference standard.

11. **Light palettes — 7.0/10.** The dialog has clear elevation, but its internal hierarchy is inefficient. The large title band and generous content gaps give repeated preset restrictions more room than the actual editing task. The swatch outline is excessively heavy. The disabled color field still resembles the primary editing affordance, with the required duplication action relegated below it. “For new voxels only” is important behavioral information, yet it sits far from Use palette and looks like incidental metadata.

12. **Light share — 6.9/10.** Surface separation is stronger here, but the selected Can remix segment is too subtle for a consequential access setting. Public access and individual permissions need clearer grouping and an explicit account of how they interact. Presence subtitles consume substantial space without helping complete sharing. The raw URL, full-width Copied button, and confirmation sentence form three consecutive rows around one action. The panel feels inflated, particularly while covering the agent session.

13. **Light tools — 6.7/10.** Readability improves, but the popup still has the wrong internal structure: a tool menu abruptly becomes a settings form. The large unused leading gutter makes built-in tools feel incomplete beside the checked custom entry. The selected custom row uses both a checkmark and a prominent outline, giving it unnecessary visual emphasis. Density, Seed, and scope do not form a compact, aligned settings group. Repeated agent attribution adds clutter without explaining behavior.

14. **Light main — 7.3/10.** The canvas gives the artwork breathing room, and the overall contrast is more controlled. The sidebar still lacks a decisive hierarchy between session identity, current activity, and completed changes. Captured-view thumbnails are miniature illustrations when they should support inspection. Layer selection is much weaker than palette selection. The cool-to-warm sidebar tint looks decorative, and several unrelated overflow buttons have identical visual prominence. The persistent “Local concept preview” footer adds prototype noise to the product composition.

**Overall score: 6.9/10.** A credible visual direction with unfinished interaction hierarchy. Additional polish should come from clearer states and tighter component behavior, not additional decoration.

Prioritized fixes:

1. **Resolve ambiguous modes and permissions.** Define editing behavior during history preview, identify what “Save current” saves, and clarify the relationship between public-link capabilities and individual access.
2. **Rebuild the session hierarchy.** Group task and agent identity, make the current operation immediately legible, and subordinate completed steps while retaining tool calls, diff counts, per-step undo, follow controls, and captured views.
3. **Standardize selection and focus.** Give layers, history rows, swatches, tabs, menu parents, and keyboard focus consistent, distinguishable treatments. Current emphasis is distributed arbitrarily.
4. **Simplify comment inspection.** Keep sidebar summaries compact when a thread is open, shorten the anchor connection, and use an annotation treatment that cannot be mistaken for voxel selection.
5. **Separate tool choice from configuration.** Make the picker a compact menu and place brush settings in a clearly bounded contextual area. Remove repeated extension labels.
6. **Tighten palettes and sharing.** Remove repeated explanations and feedback, reduce empty dialog space, and put behavioral consequences beside the relevant action.
7. **Unify materials and improve dark-mode definition.** Use neutral surfaces, consistent hairlines, and deliberate elevation. Reduce decorative tinting and improve the readability of secondary controls.
8. **Make inspection surfaces useful.** Enlarge artwork within captured-view tiles, give history a clear scroll region, and show collaborator identity where provenance matters.
